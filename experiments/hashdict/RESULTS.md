# A struct-backed hash dict: what it costs, and what a size hint buys

From `bench.txt`, generated by `./run.sh` (`-count=10 -benchtime=100ms`).
Provenance in that file's header; a QEMU guest, so **ratios are the durable
result**. Zero-cost baseline `BenchmarkNoOp` = 0.24 ns, well below every
measurement here.

## 1. Wrapping a map in a struct is nearly free

`Map[K, V]` is `map[K]V` with methods. `HashDict[K, V]` is a struct holding one,
with `noCopy`, pointer receivers, and lazy initialisation on first write.

| operation | raw builtin | `Map` | `HashDict` | HashDict vs raw |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Get` | 3.31 ns | 3.43 ns | 3.70 ns | +0.39 ns |
| `Set` | 4.87 ns | 4.89 ns | 5.40 ns | **+0.53 ns** |
| `Delete` | 4.72 ns | 4.77 ns | 4.84 ns | +0.12 ns |

**0.1–0.5 ns per operation**, 3–11%. The largest gap is `Set`, and it is the nil
check that lazily initialises the map — which is precisely the property the
struct exists to provide. You are paying half a nanosecond for the feature, not
for the wrapper.

## 2. A size hint pays far more for a map than it did for a slice

Building n entries with and without `make(map[K]V, n)`:

| n | unsized | sized | speedup | unsized B/op | sized B/op |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 528 ns | 218 ns | 2.4x | 1 128 | 664 |
| 256 | 8 348 ns | 2 054 ns | **4.1x** | 19 048 | 9 560 |
| 4 096 | 135 547 ns | 37 599 ns | 3.6x | 296 122 | 147 824 |
| 65 536 | 2 447 936 ns | 870 448 ns | 2.8x | 4 729 548 | 2 364 596 |

**2.4–4.1x faster and about half the allocation volume**, at every size.

This is the important contrast with `experiments/sizedcollect/`, which found a
size hint worth only 0–10% in wall time for the sorted containers. That was not
because hints are weak; it was because an O(n log n) sort followed and dominated.
A hash dict has no sort, so the hint is the whole difference and the saving
survives intact.

## Conclusions

Durable:

1. A struct wrapper over a map costs 3–11% per operation, most of it the
   lazy-init check that buys a usable zero value.
2. Presizing a map is worth 2.4–4.1x in time and ~2x in allocation volume.
3. **The sized-constructor argument is much stronger for a hash dict than for
   the sorted containers.** ADR 0006's 0–10% result does not carry over, and
   assuming it would have been wrong.
4. Presizing only applies at construction. Go exposes no growth hint for an
   existing map, so a bulk insert into a non-empty dict has nothing to presize.

Perishable: every absolute number above.
