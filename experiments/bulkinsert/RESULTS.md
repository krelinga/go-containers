# Bulk insertion into a sorted slice

From `bench.txt`, generated by `./run.sh` (`-count=10 -benchtime=100ms`).
Provenance is in that file's header; a QEMU guest, so absolutes are soft and
**ratios are the durable result**.

Two strategies for adding k entries to a sorted slice of n:

- **naive** — repeated `Set`: binary search plus `slices.Insert`, O(kn) in bytes moved.
- **bulk** — sort the k additions once, then merge two sorted runs, O(k log k + n + k).

Both produce a new backing array, so the comparison is like for like. A real
in-place `SetAll` would save the naive side its base clone, shifting the
crossover upward somewhat. That does not change the decision, because ADR 0004
adds no threshold to be sensitive to it.

## Bulk wins from roughly k=4–16, and the gap widens without bound

| n | k=1 | k=4 | k=16 | k=64 | k=256 | k=1024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 000 | naive 1.0x | naive 1.1x | **bulk 1.5x** | bulk 3.3x | bulk 4.4x | bulk 3.0x |
| 10 000 | naive 1.4x | **bulk 1.1x** | bulk 1.6x | bulk 2.7x | bulk 8.3x | bulk 15x |
| 100 000 | bulk 1.4x | bulk 1.8x | bulk 2.5x | bulk 5.6x | bulk 13.9x | **bulk 42x** |

**The crossover is around k≈4–16 and barely moves with n.** Below it the two are
within ~1.4x either way; above it naive degrades linearly in k while bulk stays
near-flat, because the merge is dominated by the O(n) copy that k barely affects.

## The sort inside bulk is cheap, but not free

`slices.SortStableFunc` on already-ascending input, against a bare copy of the
same data and against random input:

| k | copy only | already sorted | random |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 4 ns | 32 ns | 130 ns |
| 256 | 27 ns | 590 ns | 8 421 ns |
| 4 096 | 760 ns | 9 141 ns | 374 628 ns |

Sorting sorted input costs **~12–22x a bare copy** — not free, as an earlier
draft of ADR 0004 claimed — but **4x to 41x less than sorting random input**, and
the gap grows with k.

What matters is its share of the whole operation, and that depends entirely on
whether n dominates:

- **Merging into a large map** (n=100 000, k=4 096): the sort is ~9 µs against a
  merge of ~340 µs — about **2.7%**. A "caller promises sorted" fast path would
  save almost nothing.
- **Constructing from scratch** (n=0, k=4 096): the merge degenerates to a copy
  of ~760 ns, so the sort is roughly **90%** of the work. Here a fast path would
  save most of it.

## Conclusions

Durable:

1. Sort-then-merge beats repeated insertion from about k=4–16 upward, at every n
   measured, and the advantage grows without bound (42x at n=100 000, k=1 024).
2. The crossover is nearly independent of n.
3. Sorting already-sorted input is 4–41x cheaper than sorting random input but
   still an order of magnitude above a bare copy.
4. A pre-sorted fast path is worth ~2.7% when merging into a large map and ~90%
   when constructing from nothing. **If one is ever added, construction is the
   case that justifies it — not merging.**

Perishable: every absolute number above.
