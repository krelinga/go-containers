# What an unsized iterator costs when building a container

From `bench.txt`, generated by `./run.sh` (`-count=10 -benchtime=100ms`).
Provenance in that file's header; a QEMU guest, so **ratios are durable and
absolutes are soft**.

`iter.Seq` is a function and carries no length, so draining it uses a bare
append. Compared against appending into a slice preallocated to the exact
length, and against cloning a slice directly — the ceiling, with no iterator at
all.

## Append growth reallocates a lot, and overshoots

| n | reallocations | final cap | wasted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 3 | 16 | 0 |
| 256 | 9 | 256 | 0 |
| 4 096 | 16 | 5 120 | **+1 024 (25%)** |
| 65 536 | 26 | 69 632 | +4 096 (6%) |

## Collection alone: 3.5x to 6.4x, and up to 5x the memory

| n | unknown | known | clone | unknown allocs | known allocs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 82 ns / 248 B | 24 ns / 128 B | 24 ns | 5 | 1 |
| 256 | 706 ns / 4 088 B | 312 ns / 2 048 B | 283 ns | 9 | 1 |
| 4 096 | 13.1 µs / 128 KB | 3.1 µs / 32 KB | 3.0 µs | 16 | 1 |
| 65 536 | 321 µs / 2.5 MB | 50 µs / 524 KB | 32 µs | 26 | 1 |
| 1 048 576 | 3 484 µs / 41.7 MB | 562 µs / 8.4 MB | 425 µs | 38 | 1 |

**Time 3.5–6.4x. Allocation volume up to 5x.** A preallocated collect is within
~30% of a bare `slices.Clone`, so knowing the length recovers nearly all of it.

## But end to end, the time difference almost vanishes

The same three strategies followed by the sort and dedup that actually build a
sorted set:

| n | unknown | known | ratio | unknown bytes | known bytes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 115 ns | 55 ns | 2.09x | 248 B | 128 B |
| 256 | 1 670 ns | 1 327 ns | 1.26x | 4 088 B | 2 048 B |
| 4 096 | 88.1 µs | 88.1 µs | **1.00x** | 128 KB | 32 KB |
| 65 536 | 3 247 µs | 2 946 µs | 1.10x | 2.5 MB | 524 KB |
| 1 048 576 | 61.4 ms | 58.5 ms | 1.05x | 41.7 MB | 8.4 MB |

**The O(n log n) sort dominates.** Above a few hundred elements the wall-clock
difference is 0–10%, and at n=4 096 it is unmeasurable.

**The allocation gap does not shrink.** Building a one-million-element sorted set
churns 41.7 MB instead of 8.4 MB — 5x the garbage for a 5% time saving.

## Conclusions

Durable:

1. Draining an unsized `iter.Seq` costs 3.5–6.4x the time and up to 5x the
   allocation volume of a length-known collect, across four orders of magnitude.
2. A preallocated collect is within ~30% of a bare slice clone, so a size hint
   recovers nearly all of the loss.
3. **Where a sort follows, the wall-clock benefit is 0–10% above a few hundred
   elements**, because the sort dominates. Every collect in this library is
   followed by a sort.
4. The allocation-volume gap survives the sort intact, at up to 5x.

Perishable: every absolute number above.

**A size hint is worth having for allocation pressure, not for wall-clock time.**
That argues for the cheapest mechanism that works, not an elaborate one — the
saving does not fund much API surface.
